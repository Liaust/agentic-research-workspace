# Orientation protocol v2

Inspect the complete PDF before proposing a reading plan. The complete paper
will remain available to every later reading job; orientation does not crop,
parse, summarize away, or replace the source.

First identify coherent source scopes whose union covers every page. Scopes are
context routes, not units of extraction, and may overlap where a transition or
thread needs context.

Then inventory source-native landmarks that require an explicit later
disposition. Include definitions, assumptions, questions, cited or reported
positions, conceptual distinctions, equations, figures, methods, results,
objections, responses, qualifications, transitions, and conclusions when they
are substantive in this source. A landmark is a reading checkpoint, not yet a
canonical atom. Use the source's structure and vocabulary; do not force every
paper into the same landmark mix.

Create bounded reading batches that group related landmarks into coherent work.
Prefer batches small enough that the reader can inspect, represent, and account
for every assigned landmark in one proposal. Each landmark must belong to
exactly one batch. A batch may reference several context scopes and each scope
may support several batches.

Sketch scientific threads in source presentation order and associate each
thread with its relevant scopes and landmarks. The plan is revisable guidance,
not canonical output. Record source-native type pressure and warnings.

Set `manual_review` only when the source is inaccessible, corrupt, missing
material needed to plan, or contains an ambiguity that ordinary agent
inspection cannot resolve. Do not request manual review merely because later
reading must render, zoom, transcribe, or visually verify equations, figures,
symbols, or a noisy text layer; those are normal reading and audit duties and
belong in `warnings`. Do not extract canonical records, use another source, or
perform field-level synthesis during orientation.
